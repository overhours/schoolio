01 · The foundations

Wave basics

Start here. These definitions are the building blocks of the whole topic.

Learn the language
〰

What is a wave?

A wave is a way of transferring energy from one place to another without transferring matter overall.

Think of a water wave: the energy travels, but the water mostly moves around its position.
↕

Transverse waves

Oscillations are perpendicular (at right angles) to the direction the energy travels.

  • Light and all electromagnetic waves
  • Water surface waves (GCSE model)
  • Waves on a rope
↔

Longitudinal waves

Oscillations are parallel to the direction the energy travels.

  • Sound waves in air
  • Compressions: particles close together
  • Rarefactions: particles spread out

Spot the difference

The particle movement is different, but both waves transfer energy.

Wave features

  • Amplitude: maximum displacement from rest position.
  • Wavelength (λ): distance between matching points, e.g. crest to crest.
  • Frequency (f): number of waves passing a point each second.
  • Period (T): time for one complete wave.
02 · Maths you need

The wave equation

Learn it, rearrange it, use it. Units matter in exam questions.

High-value exam skill

Wave speed = frequency × wavelength

v = f × λ
v = wave speed (m/s)f = frequency (Hz)λ = wavelength (m)
Rearrange it: f = v ÷ λ   |   λ = v ÷ f

Worked example

A wave has a frequency of 5 Hz and a wavelength of 2 m. Calculate its speed.

Step 1: Write the equation: v = f × λ

Step 2: Substitute: v = 5 × 2

Answer: 10 m/s

Frequency and period

T = 1 ÷ f

Period is measured in seconds (s). Frequency is measured in hertz (Hz).

Quick calculation

If frequency = 20 Hz:

T = 1 ÷ 20 = 0.05 s

One complete wave takes 0.05 seconds.

Exam technique

  • Write the equation first.
  • Convert units if needed.
  • Show substitution and working.
  • Give the correct unit.
03 · Electromagnetic waves

The EM spectrum

All electromagnetic waves are transverse and travel at the same speed in a vacuum.

Memorise the order

From longest wavelength to shortest

RadioBroadcasting
MicrowavesCooking
InfraredHeaters
VisibleSeeing
UltravioletSecurity
X-raysImaging
GammaSterilising
← Longer wavelength · lower frequencyShorter wavelength · higher frequency →
Mnemonic: Ready Medics In Vans Use X-ray Goggles. (Radio → Gamma)

Uses

  • Radio: radio and TV communication
  • Microwaves: satellite communication and cooking
  • Infrared: thermal imaging and remote controls
  • Visible: photography and fibre optics
  • Ultraviolet: fluorescent lamps and security marking
  • X-rays: medical imaging
  • Gamma: sterilising medical equipment and treating cancer

Dangers

All EM waves can transfer energy. The higher-frequency waves (UV, X-rays and gamma) can damage cells and DNA and may cause mutations or cancer.

  • UV can damage skin and eyes.
  • X-rays can damage cells.
  • Gamma rays are highly penetrating and ionising.

Key properties

  • All are transverse waves.
  • All travel at 300,000,000 m/s in a vacuum.
  • They can travel through a vacuum.
  • They transfer energy.
  • Higher frequency means higher energy.
04 · Wave behaviour

Reflection & refraction

Know what happens when waves meet a surface or change medium.

↩

Reflection

A wave bounces off a surface. The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, measured from the normal (a line at 90° to the surface).

⤴

Refraction

A wave changes direction when it enters a different medium because its speed changes.

  • Frequency stays the same.
  • Wavelength changes.
  • Slows down → bends towards the normal.
  • Speeds up → bends away from the normal.
◎

Diffraction

Waves spread out when they pass through a gap or around an obstacle.

  • Most noticeable when gap size is similar to wavelength.
  • Longer wavelengths diffract more for the same gap.
  • Sound can be heard around corners due to diffraction.
05 · Sound waves

Sound & ultrasound

Sound is a longitudinal wave that needs a medium to travel through.

Hearing sound

Sound waves are vibrations that travel through particles in a medium. They cannot travel through a vacuum because there are no particles to vibrate.

  • Higher frequency = higher pitch.
  • Greater amplitude = louder sound.
  • Human hearing is approximately 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz.

Ultrasound

Ultrasound has a frequency above 20,000 Hz, so humans cannot hear it.

  • Medical scans: echoes create images of organs and unborn babies.
  • Industrial testing: detects cracks inside materials.
  • Sonar: measures distances underwater.

Echo method

A pulse travels to a surface and reflects back. The measured time is for the journey there and back.

distance = speed × time ÷ 2

Remember to divide by 2 to get the one-way distance.
